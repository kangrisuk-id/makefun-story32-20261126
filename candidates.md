# Candidates — "Tell a Whole Story in 32 Characters"

Judge criteria (in order, per kit brief): **1. Joke** (lands on first read, clear twist/punchline)
→ **2. Story** (situation + character + something happened) → **3. Freshness** (avoids stock
Wi-Fi jokes) → **4. Tight** (every character earns its place).

Scoring: Joke /10 + Story /7 + Freshness /5 + Tight /3 = **/25**.
All lengths verified with `len()` in Python. Stock jokes deliberately avoided or twisted:
"FBI Surveillance Van", "Pretty Fly for a Wi-Fi", "It Burns When IP", "Tell My WiFi Love Her",
"Skynet", "The LAN Before Time", "Abraham Linksys", "Martin Router King", "LAN Solo",
"Connectile Dysfunction", "Nacho WiFi", "Virus.exe", "404 Not Found".

Devices covered: divorce settlement, ex-password revenge, router-as-witness, neighbour feud,
landlord/rent, parent grounding, cat sabotage, work-from-home, ghost, password-as-date.

## Knockout tournament (top 8 seeds)

**Seeds:** S1 "The WiFi died with the marriage" (23) · S2 "He took the couch, I kept WiFi" (23) ·
S3 "The router knows what you did" (22) · S4 "Password: the day you left me" (22) ·
S5 "She took the dog, not the router" (21) · S6 "Your ex changed the password" (21) ·
S7 "Ask Dave, he's still paying" (21) · S8 "Signal's strong, marriage isn't" (21).

### Quarter-finals
- **QF1 — S1 vs S8:** S1 wins. Joke: "died with" is one sharp twist that lands instantly;
  S8's "isn't" is a flat contrast with no punchline. First differing criterion (joke) decides.
- **QF2 — S2 vs S7:** S2 wins. Story: a two-beat divorce settlement (couch vs WiFi, characters +
  outcome) beats S7's one-beat leech gag; S7 never shows who Dave is to the speaker.
- **QF3 — S3 vs S6:** S3 wins. Freshness + joke: the menacing mystery ("what you did") is
  fresher than the familiar ex-changed-the-password trope; S6's twist is predictable.
- **QF4 — S4 vs S5:** S4 wins. Story + joke: "the day you left me" as a password carries the
  whole breakup — the exact day, who left, the wound — in five words; S5's dog-vs-router
  preference gag is funny but thinner as a story.

### Semi-finals
- **SF1 — S1 vs S4:** S1 wins, narrowly. Joke: S1's twist (the WiFi as a divorce casualty)
  surprises more than S4's password-as-date device, which is a known pattern. Story: both carry
  a full marriage arc; S4 is slightly more specific (the exact day) but S1's "died with" implies
  the death of the whole marriage, not just the departure. Freshness breaks the near-tie for S1.
- **SF2 — S2 vs S3:** S2 wins. Story (criterion 3): S2 shows two characters and a settlement
  scene; S3 implies a secret but never says what happened — the reader cannot fill in the rest.
  S3's menace is funny, S2's scene is a whole story.

### Final
- **S1 "The WiFi died with the marriage" vs S2 "He took the couch, I kept WiFi":**
  - Joke: S1 edges it — "died with" is a single sharp twist, first-read punch; S2's humour is
    a priorities gag ("WiFi > couch") that needs a half-beat more setup.
  - Story: near-even — S2 has two characters and a settlement; S1 has a marriage and a death.
    S2 is marginally more scenic, S1 marginally more complete (beginning → end of a marriage).
  - Freshness: even — neither is a stock Wi-Fi joke.
  - Tight: even — 31 vs 30 chars, no wasted word in either.
  - **Winner: S1, by the first differing criterion (joke), margin 2/5.**

## Self-pairwise (winner vs the 2 runners-up, criterion by criterion)

- **Winner vs runner-up S2 "He took the couch, I kept WiFi":** joke → S1 (sharper twist);
  story → near-even, S2 slightly more scenic; freshness → even; tight → even.
  S1 wins on the first differing criterion.
- **Winner vs 3rd place S3 "The router knows what you did":** joke → S1 (clear punchline vs
  menace without a punch); story → S1 (a marriage's whole arc vs an unspecified secret);
  freshness → S3 slightly (menace is rarer) but not by enough to overturn criteria 1–2.
  S1 wins.

## WINNER

**"The WiFi died with the marriage" — 31 characters — 23/25**

- Joke (9/10): the WiFi is a casualty of the divorce — deadpan, one twist, first-read.
- Story (7/7): a whole marriage arc — it lived, it ended, the network outlived nothing.
- Freshness (4/5): not a stock Wi-Fi joke; no FBI van, no "Pretty Fly", no password pun.
- Tight (3/3): six words, no filler; "died with" does all the work.
